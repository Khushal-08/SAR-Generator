PREVENTION OF MONEY LAUNDERING ACT (PMLA) - KEY PROVISIONS
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

This document provides essential PMLA provisions and RBI guidelines relevant
to Suspicious Activity Report (SAR) generation.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
PMLA SECTION 12: OBLIGATION TO FURNISH INFORMATION
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

REQUIREMENT:
Every banking company, financial institution, and intermediary SHALL furnish
information of transactions, whether attempted or executed, to the Director,
Financial Intelligence Unit-India (FIU-IND).

TIMELINE:
• Suspicious Transaction Report (STR): Within 7 days of forming suspicion
• Cash Transaction Report (CTR): Within 15 days of transaction month end

PENALTY FOR NON-COMPLIANCE:
• Fine up to ₹1 lakh per day of default
• Criminal liability for willful non-compliance
• Regulatory action by RBI/SEBI/IRDAI

KEY PROVISIONS:
✓ Obligation applies regardless of transaction amount for suspicious activity
✓ Includes attempted transactions, not just completed ones
✓ Protected disclosure - no civil/criminal liability for good faith reporting
✓ Tipping off prohibited - cannot inform customer about SAR filing

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
PMLA SECTION 13: OBLIGATION TO VERIFY IDENTITY
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

REQUIREMENT:
Banking companies and financial institutions SHALL maintain records and 
verify identity of clients under Know Your Customer (KYC) norms.

KEY PROVISIONS:
✓ Identity verification at account opening
✓ Periodic updating of KYC information
✓ Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD) for high-risk customers
✓ Politically Exposed Persons (PEPs) require senior management approval

RECORDS TO BE MAINTAINED:
• Identity proof (Aadhaar, PAN, Passport, etc.)
• Address verification documents
• Photographs
• Account opening forms with signature
• Beneficial ownership information (for entities)

RETENTION PERIOD:
Minimum 5 years after business relationship ends or transaction completion

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
PMLA RULE 3: CASH TRANSACTION REPORT (CTR)
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

THRESHOLD:
• Cash transactions of ₹10 lakhs or more (single transaction)
• Series of cash transactions integrally connected totaling ₹10 lakhs or more
• Applies per account per month

REPORTING:
Must be reported within 15 days of month end to FIU-IND

STRUCTURING TO AVOID:
Multiple transactions designed to stay below threshold are themselves
suspicious and must be reported as STR under Section 12

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
RBI MASTER DIRECTION ON KYC - KEY HIGHLIGHTS
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

RISK-BASED APPROACH:
Banks must categorize customers into risk categories:
• Low Risk: Salaried employees, government departments
• Medium Risk: Regular customers with standard profiles
• High Risk: PEPs, non-resident customers, trust/NGO accounts

CUSTOMER DUE DILIGENCE (CDD):
SIMPLIFIED DUE DILIGENCE (for low-risk):
• Basic identity and address verification
• Normal monitoring frequency

ENHANCED DUE DILIGENCE (for high-risk):
• Senior management approval required
• Source of funds and wealth verification
• Purpose and nature of business relationship
• Ongoing monitoring with increased frequency
• Country risk assessment for cross-border transactions

POLITICALLY EXPOSED PERSONS (PEP):
DEFINITION:
• Individuals entrusted with prominent public functions
• Includes heads of state, senior politicians, judicial/military officers
• Extends to immediate family members and close associates

SPECIAL REQUIREMENTS:
✓ Obtain senior management approval before establishing relationship
✓ Take reasonable measures to establish source of wealth and funds
✓ Conduct enhanced ongoing monitoring

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SUSPICIOUS TRANSACTION REPORTING - RBI GUIDELINES
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

WHAT MAKES A TRANSACTION SUSPICIOUS:
A transaction is suspicious if it:
1. Gives rise to reasonable grounds to believe it may involve proceeds of crime
2. Appears to be made in circumstances of unusual or unjustified complexity
3. Appears to have no economic rationale or bona fide purpose
4. Is inconsistent with the customer's known profile or business
5. Involves jurisdictions known for high money laundering risk

RED FLAGS FOR SUSPICION:
Transaction Patterns:
⚠ Structuring to avoid reporting thresholds
⚠ Rapid movement of funds (in and immediate out)
⚠ Transactions just below significant threshold amounts
⚠ Uncharacteristic transactions for customer profile

Customer Behavior:
⚠ Reluctance to provide information or documentation
⚠ Multiple accounts with no apparent purpose
⚠ Frequent changes in payee information
⚠ Transactions involving uncooperative customers

Cross-Border Transactions:
⚠ Transfers to/from high-risk jurisdictions
⚠ No apparent business reason for foreign transactions
⚠ Circular movement of funds across borders
⚠ Trade transactions with inflated/deflated values

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
FATF RECOMMENDATIONS (APPLICABLE TO INDIA)
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

India is a member of FATF (Financial Action Task Force) and implements
its 40 Recommendations on anti-money laundering:

KEY RECOMMENDATIONS FOR SAR CONTEXT:

RECOMMENDATION 10 - CUSTOMER DUE DILIGENCE:
• Identify customer and verify identity using reliable documents
• Identify beneficial owner and verify identity
• Understand purpose and nature of business relationship
• Conduct ongoing due diligence

RECOMMENDATION 11 - RECORD KEEPING:
• Maintain records for at least 5 years
• Sufficient to permit reconstruction of transactions
• Available to competent authorities upon request

RECOMMENDATION 12 - POLITICALLY EXPOSED PERSONS:
• Risk management systems to determine if customer is PEP
• Senior management approval for relationships with PEPs
• Enhanced due diligence measures

RECOMMENDATION 15 - NEW TECHNOLOGIES:
• Identify and assess ML/TF risks of new products and technologies
• Applies to virtual assets and virtual asset service providers

RECOMMENDATION 16 - WIRE TRANSFERS:
• Obtain and maintain required originator and beneficiary information
• Cross-border transfers require full information

RECOMMENDATION 20 - REPORTING OF SUSPICIOUS TRANSACTIONS:
• Report suspicious transactions promptly to FIU
• Protected disclosure - no liability for good faith reporting
• Prohibition on tipping off

RECOMMENDATION 22 - CASINO SECTOR:
• Apply CDD measures to casino customers
• Monitor and report suspicious transactions

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SAR NARRATIVE BEST PRACTICES
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

STRUCTURE:
Paragraph 1 - WHAT HAPPENED:
• Factual description of transactions
• Amounts, dates, parties involved
• Sequence of events

Paragraph 2 - WHY IT IS SUSPICIOUS:
• Comparison to customer profile
• Identification of red flags
• Inconsistencies with stated purpose

Paragraph 3 - REGULATORY ANALYSIS:
• Money laundering typology identification
• Citation of relevant PMLA sections and FATF recommendations
• Assessment of potential criminal proceeds

LANGUAGE REQUIREMENTS:
✓ Objective and factual (avoid speculation)
✓ Clear and concise (avoid jargon unless necessary)
✓ Properly cite regulations (PMLA Section X, FATF Rec Y)
✓ Use regulatory terminology correctly
✓ Avoid accusatory language (use "indicates" not "proves")

CRITICAL ELEMENTS TO INCLUDE:
1. Transaction timeline with specific dates
2. Amounts in numerical form with currency
3. Customer profile summary
4. Identified red flags with explanations
5. Typology match with supporting rationale
6. Regulatory citations
7. Recommendation for SAR filing

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
PROTECTED DISCLOSURE & TIPPING OFF
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

LEGAL PROTECTION (PMLA Section 12(1B)):
A report made in good faith under Section 12 shall not constitute a breach of:
• Banking confidentiality obligations
• Any duty of secrecy
• Any contractual obligation
No civil or criminal liability for good faith disclosure

TIPPING OFF PROHIBITION (PMLA Section 12(2)):
It is PROHIBITED to:
• Inform the customer that an STR has been filed
• Disclose any information that might prejudice an investigation
• Tip off the subject of suspicion in any manner

PENALTY FOR TIPPING OFF:
• Imprisonment up to 7 years
• Fine up to ₹5 lakhs
• Both imprisonment and fine

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
RECORD RETENTION REQUIREMENTS
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

MINIMUM RETENTION: 5 years from cessation of relationship

RECORDS TO MAINTAIN:
• All transaction records (statements, receipts, invoices)
• KYC documentation (identity, address, photograph)
• Account opening forms and correspondence
• Risk categorization and review records
• Enhanced due diligence documentation
• SAR filing records and supporting documents

FORMAT:
• Original documents or certified copies
• Electronic records acceptable if retrievable and auditable
• Must permit reconstruction of transaction trail
