MONEY LAUNDERING TYPOLOGIES - REFERENCE GUIDE FOR SAR ANALYSIS
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

This document provides detailed descriptions of common money laundering 
typologies for use in Suspicious Activity Report (SAR) narrative generation.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
1. STRUCTURING / SMURFING
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
DEFINITION:
Breaking large transactions into multiple smaller amounts to avoid mandatory 
reporting thresholds (CTR threshold in India: ₹10 lakhs for cash transactions).

TYPICAL PATTERNS:
• Multiple deposits/withdrawals just below ₹20,000 (cash transaction reporting limit)
• Consistent small amounts across multiple days
• Use of multiple individuals ("smurfs") to make deposits
• Geographic spread across different branches/ATMs to avoid detection
• Timing pattern designed to evade automated monitoring

RED FLAGS:
⚠ Deposits consistently below reporting thresholds (e.g., ₹19,500, ₹19,800)
⚠ Multiple transactions in same day at different locations
⚠ No credible explanation for cash source
⚠ Pattern emerges over weeks/months
⚠ Customer has no business justifying cash handling

REGULATORY FRAMEWORK:
• PMLA Section 12 - Obligation to report suspicious transactions
• Rule 3 of PMLA Rules - Cash Transaction Report (CTR) requirements
• FATF Recommendation 10 - Customer due diligence
• RBI Master Direction on KYC - Enhanced monitoring requirements

NARRATIVE LANGUAGE:
"The transaction pattern exhibits characteristics consistent with structuring, 
wherein multiple deposits were made in amounts deliberately designed to avoid 
triggering cash transaction reporting requirements under PMLA Rule 3."

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
2. RAPID FUNDS MOVEMENT / LAYERING
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
DEFINITION:
Quick movement of funds through accounts with no economic purpose, designed 
to distance money from its illicit source and complicate audit trails.

TYPICAL PATTERNS:
• Funds received and immediately transferred out (same day or within 24 hours)
• No accumulation of balance in the account
• Multiple rapid transfers through different accounts
• Use of intermediary accounts to create complexity
• Cross-border transfers to increase distance from source

RED FLAGS:
⚠ Credits followed immediately by debits of similar amounts
⚠ No legitimate business purpose for transactions
⚠ Account used as mere conduit or "pass-through"
⚠ Minimal balance retention
⚠ Pattern of in-and-out transactions

REGULATORY FRAMEWORK:
• FATF Recommendation 10 - Customer due diligence and monitoring
• FATF Recommendation 16 - Wire transfers
• PMLA Section 12 - Suspicious transaction reporting

NARRATIVE LANGUAGE:
"The account exhibited rapid funds movement characteristic of layering, wherein 
credits were systematically followed by immediate debits with no accumulation 
of funds or apparent economic purpose, suggesting an attempt to distance the 
funds from their source and complicate transaction tracing."

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
3. TRADE-BASED MONEY LAUNDERING (TBML)
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
DEFINITION:
Misuse of trade transactions to disguise illicit funds, typically through 
manipulation of price, quantity, or quality of imports/exports.

TYPICAL PATTERNS:
• Over-invoicing or under-invoicing of goods
• Multiple payments from unrelated parties for single trade
• Shipment of goods at prices significantly above/below market value
• Phantom shipping (no actual goods movement)
• Over/under-shipment of goods relative to invoice
• Falsified documents or descriptions

RED FLAGS:
⚠ Transaction volume inconsistent with business size
⚠ Payments from multiple third parties for single transaction
⚠ Immediate cross-border transfers following receipts
⚠ Invoice prices 2-3x market value
⚠ No supporting documentation (bills of lading, customs clearance)
⚠ Customer has no history in stated trade

REGULATORY FRAMEWORK:
• FATF Recommendation 10 - Customer due diligence
• FATF Recommendation 16 - Wire transfers
• FATF Best Practices on TBML (2008, updated 2020)
• Indian Customs Act provisions on valuation

NARRATIVE LANGUAGE:
"The transaction pattern is indicative of trade-based money laundering, wherein 
the customer received multiple payments from unrelated third parties followed by 
immediate cross-border transfers, with invoice valuations significantly exceeding 
market prices for the stated goods, and lacking credible documentation to support 
the legitimacy of the trade activity."

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
4. ROUND-TRIPPING
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
DEFINITION:
Transferring funds to foreign jurisdictions and then routing them back to the 
country of origin through multiple channels to disguise their source and 
appear as foreign investment or legitimate foreign income.

TYPICAL PATTERNS:
• Wire transfer to offshore entity
• Funds return through different route (different entity, different account)
• Multiple jurisdictions involved to add complexity
• Same ultimate beneficial owner on both sides
• Returns may be disguised as loans, investments, or gifts

RED FLAGS:
⚠ Circular flow of funds
⚠ Use of tax havens or secrecy jurisdictions
⚠ Related party transactions disguised as arm's length
⚠ No commercial rationale for routing
⚠ Returns within short timeframe

REGULATORY FRAMEWORK:
• FATF Recommendation 10, 16 - Customer due diligence and wire transfers
• FEMA regulations on capital account transactions
• Income Tax Act provisions on deemed income
• PMLA Section 12

NARRATIVE LANGUAGE:
"The transactions exhibit a round-tripping pattern wherein funds were transferred 
to offshore entities in jurisdictions known for banking secrecy, and subsequently 
returned to India through circuitous routes involving multiple intermediaries, 
with no demonstrable commercial purpose for such complex structuring, suggesting 
an attempt to disguise the domestic origin of funds and potentially evade 
taxation or regulatory oversight."

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
5. CASH-INTENSIVE BUSINESS LAUNDERING
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
DEFINITION:
Mixing illicit funds with legitimate cash-intensive business receipts to 
disguise the source of criminal proceeds.

TYPICAL PATTERNS:
• Cash deposits significantly exceeding reported sales
• Business type allows for revenue inflation (restaurants, retail, services)
• Deposits inconsistent with business size, location, or customer traffic
• No corresponding increase in expenses, inventory, or employees
• Minimal card/digital transactions despite modern payment trends

RED FLAGS:
⚠ Cash deposits 50-200% above reported revenues
⚠ No credible explanation for excess cash
⚠ Deposits made at unusual times or frequencies
⚠ Business has minimal physical presence or operations
⚠ Revenue inconsistent with observable business activity

REGULATORY FRAMEWORK:
• PMLA Section 12 - Suspicious transaction reporting
• FATF Recommendation 10 - Customer due diligence
• Income Tax Act Section 269ST - Cash transaction limits
• RBI guidelines on cash handling

NARRATIVE LANGUAGE:
"The cash deposit pattern indicates potential laundering through a cash-intensive 
business, wherein deposits substantially exceed the declared sales revenue without 
corresponding increases in operational indicators, suggesting the commingling of 
illicit funds with legitimate business receipts to create an appearance of 
lawful origin."

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
6. FUNNEL ACCOUNT / COLLECTION ACCOUNT
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
DEFINITION:
An account that receives funds from multiple sources and consolidates them 
for transfer to a single destination, often with no legitimate purpose.

TYPICAL PATTERNS:
• Multiple small credits from many different individuals/entities
• Single large debit consolidating all credits
• Account holder has no business justifying such activity
• Credits from geographically dispersed sources
• Consistent pattern repeated regularly

RED FLAGS:
⚠ 50+ individual creditors to single account
⚠ Account holder is unemployed or has inconsistent profile
⚠ Funds transferred to shell companies or offshore entities
⚠ No legitimate business or family reason for collections
⚠ Account opened recently before activity began

REGULATORY FRAMEWORK:
• FATF Recommendation 10 - Customer due diligence
• PMLA Section 12 - Suspicious transaction reporting
• RBI KYC guidelines on purpose of account

NARRATIVE LANGUAGE:
"The account exhibits characteristics of a funnel account, receiving funds from 
89 disparate sources and systematically consolidating them for transfer to a 
single beneficiary, with the account holder's profile providing no legitimate 
explanation for such collection and disbursement activity, suggesting potential 
use as an intermediary in a larger money laundering scheme."

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
7. SHELL COMPANY TRANSACTIONS
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
DEFINITION:
Use of companies with no genuine business operations to move or store funds, 
disguising true ownership and purpose.

TYPICAL PATTERNS:
• Company has no employees or physical presence
• Registered address is virtual office or accommodation address
• No evidence of actual business operations
• Complex ownership structures obscuring beneficial owner
• Transactions inconsistent with stated business purpose
• Often combined with TBML or other typologies

RED FLAGS:
⚠ Zero employees despite large transaction volumes
⚠ No utility bills, rent payments, or operational expenses
⚠ Directors are nominees or foreign entities
⚠ Recently incorporated but immediately active with large transactions
⚠ Transactions only with related entities

REGULATORY FRAMEWORK:
• FATF Recommendation 24, 25 - Beneficial ownership transparency
• Companies Act 2013 provisions on beneficial ownership
• PMLA Rules on beneficial ownership identification
• SEBI regulations on shell companies

NARRATIVE LANGUAGE:
"The entity exhibits shell company characteristics, including absence of 
employees, lack of physical operations, use of virtual office addresses, and 
transaction patterns inconsistent with the stated business purpose, combined 
with opaque ownership structures that obscure beneficial ownership, indicating 
potential use as a vehicle for money laundering rather than legitimate 
commercial activity."

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
8. POLITICALLY EXPOSED PERSONS (PEP) CORRUPTION PROCEEDS
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
DEFINITION:
Suspicious transactions involving government officials or their family members 
that may represent proceeds of corruption, bribery, or abuse of public office.

TYPICAL PATTERNS:
• Large unexplained deposits to government official's account
• Receipts from unknown foreign sources
• Amounts grossly disproportionate to official salary
• Use of family members' accounts to disguise beneficial ownership
• Transfers from entities benefiting from government contracts

RED FLAGS:
⚠ PEP receiving funds 5-10x annual salary
⚠ Offshore transfers to/from tax havens
⚠ Timing correlates with government contract awards
⚠ Use of intermediaries to distance PEP from transaction
⚠ Stated purpose (gifts, family support) not credible given amounts

REGULATORY FRAMEWORK:
• FATF Recommendation 12, 22 - PEP due diligence
• RBI Master Direction on KYC - Enhanced Due Diligence for PEPs
• Prevention of Corruption Act
• PMLA Section 12

NARRATIVE LANGUAGE:
"The account holder, identified as a Politically Exposed Person per RBI 
guidelines, received substantial funds from foreign jurisdictions known for 
banking secrecy, with amounts grossly disproportionate to declared income and 
lacking credible justification, raising concerns regarding potential proceeds 
of corruption or abuse of public office as contemplated under FATF 
Recommendation 12 and requiring enhanced due diligence."

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
9. CRYPTOCURRENCY / VIRTUAL ASSET LAUNDERING
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
DEFINITION:
Use of cryptocurrencies or virtual assets to transfer, obscure, or legitimize 
illicit funds, often exploiting the pseudonymous nature of blockchain transactions.

TYPICAL PATTERNS:
• Rapid conversion of cash to cryptocurrency
• Use of P2P platforms to avoid KYC
• Transfers to foreign crypto exchanges
• Mixing services or tumblers to obscure transaction trail
• Conversion back to fiat through different jurisdiction

RED FLAGS:
⚠ Large fiat deposits followed by immediate crypto exchange transfers
⚠ P2P cryptocurrency transactions with multiple counterparties
⚠ No investment justification given transaction volume
⚠ Use of privacy coins (Monero, Zcash)
⚠ Pattern suggests layering through crypto

REGULATORY FRAMEWORK:
• FATF Recommendation 15 - New Technologies and Virtual Assets
• FATF Guidance on Virtual Assets and Virtual Asset Service Providers (2021)
• RBI regulations on cryptocurrency (evolving)
• PMLA applicability to virtual asset transactions

NARRATIVE LANGUAGE:
"The transaction pattern indicates potential use of virtual assets for money 
laundering purposes, with rapid conversion of fiat currency to cryptocurrency 
through international exchanges, followed by complex transaction chains designed 
to obscure the audit trail, in a manner consistent with FATF's identification 
of virtual asset risks in its Recommendation 15 guidance."

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
10. GAMBLING / CASINO LAYERING
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
DEFINITION:
Using gambling establishments to legitimize illicit funds by creating an 
appearance that winnings are the source of wealth.

TYPICAL PATTERNS:
• Large casino chip purchases with minimal actual gambling
• Quick conversion of chips back to cash or cheque
• Negligible net gambling loss (98-99% of funds returned)
• Multiple visits with same pattern
• Declared source of wealth becomes "gambling winnings"

RED FLAGS:
⚠ Casino deposits followed by immediate withdrawals
⚠ Less than 2-5% of funds actually wagered
⚠ Pattern repeated across multiple visits
⚠ Amounts inconsistent with customer profile
⚠ First-time gambler with large amounts

REGULATORY FRAMEWORK:
• FATF Recommendation 22 (previously Special Recommendation) - Casinos
• PMLA applicability to casinos
• Casino KYC and reporting requirements
• State gambling regulations

NARRATIVE LANGUAGE:
"The pattern of casino transactions indicates potential layering activity rather 
than genuine gambling, with large cash conversions to chips followed by minimal 
wagering and immediate redemption, resulting in negligible net loss while creating 
an appearance of legitimate source through casino documentation, as identified in 
FATF's casino-specific AML recommendations."
